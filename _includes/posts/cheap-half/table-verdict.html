Who or whatVerdict, and why
Physics-based design checked in a wet labHero. Computation, including free-energy calculations, solved the selectivity problem behind zasocitinib. The molecule beat the first drug in its class head to head and is now under FDA review. The software that did the calculations is sold at tool prices.
The people and companies that pay for trials and decide when to stopHero. They pay for the expensive part, where most programs fail, and trial results grade their decisions. But the judgment behind each one has never been scored, because nobody records beforehand what was known when it was made.
Chinese drugmakers that sell moleculesHero. A buyer pays them cash at signing, and the average upfront payment for a Chinese drug candidate has more than tripled since 2022.
The “AI-discovered drug” labelGimmick. Zasocitinib is the strongest molecule that computation has produced, and Nimbus’s own president of R&D would not use the term “first AI-approved drug” for it. In the 2024 count, the ten AI-designed molecules that had finished Phase 2 passed at about the historical rate.
Platforms and startups with no molecule to showGimmick, for now. Two platform deals together brought one-fiftieth of the cash that a single molecule brought. The best-funded platform raised US$2.1 billion in order to become a biotech.
Science benchmarks in model launchesGimmick. Most are question-and-answer tests of knowledge, or agent tasks that climb a leaderboard on a fixed dataset. They are built like Kaggle competitions, with the answer written down in advance. A drug is graded by patients first and then by the FDA.
AI for the decisions that cost real moneyEmpty so far. This row turns into a hero the day someone publishes a time-stamped scorecard showing a model’s calls beating people’s calls on the same decisions. It is the cheapest of the three tests listed earlier, and nobody has published one.
